Accuracy and precision: what can go wrong with a reading?

Every reading = the part's true value + the measurement error. MSA splits that error into two families.

Location = accuracy (trueness)

Is the average reading right?

Bias (at one point), linearity (bias across the range), stability (bias over time). Checked against a traceable reference value.

Width = precision

Do repeated readings agree?

Repeatability (same appraiser, same part) and reproducibility (between appraisers). This is what a gauge R&R study measures.

A note on the words
The AIAG MSA manual uses "accuracy" for the location side. The international metrology vocabulary (VIM) and ISO 5725 call that part "trueness" and use "accuracy" for trueness and precision together. Both describe the same thing; we say "accuracy (trueness)" once and "accuracy" after that.

Calibration certificate vs bias study

Is this master value trustworthy?

"The master's value is the drawing nominal."
Not traceable. The nominal is the target, not a measurement. Measure the master with a more accurate, calibrated instrument and record that value with its uncertainty.
"The master was measured once, three years ago."
Out of date. Masters wear, corrode and get dropped. Give the master its own calibration interval, like any gauge.
"The master was measured on the same type of micrometer we are studying."
Not good enough. The reference must be clearly more accurate than the gauge under study (many plants use 10 : 1, at least 4 : 1).
"The certificate is from an accredited lab, but its scope lists only pressure and temperature."
Not covered. Accreditation is per parameter and range. The lab's scope must include this kind of dimensional measurement.
"The certificate is valid, but its serial number does not match the master's ID."
No evidence for this master. The certificate belongs to another item, or the ID was changed. Same problem if the due date has passed.

The study types follow the approach of the AIAG MSA reference manual: gauge R&R by the average and range method and by ANOVA, the range method as a quick check, bias by the independent sample method (t-test), linearity by regression with confidence bands, and stability on an X̄ and R chart. Settings that are our own choice: in ANOVA the interaction is pooled when its p-value is above 0.25 (the manual advises a high significance level but gives no value); stability limits come from the first 15 checks. Acceptance guidance: %GRR under 10% acceptable; 10% to 30% may be acceptable depending on the application, cost and customer; over 30% not acceptable; ndc 5 or more. Your customer's specific requirements come first.
